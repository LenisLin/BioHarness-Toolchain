# Graph / Neighborhood

## Problem Boundary

Use this Layer 2 file after Layer 1 routes the task to `Graph / Neighborhood`.

This topic covers neighborhood, niche, microenvironment state, cellular motif, spatial factor, soft multicellular signature, reconstructed-neighborhood, and spatial-context representation tasks. These outputs are not interchangeable: niche labels, motifs, factors, embeddings, reconstructed neighborhoods, and gene representations should remain separate during selection.

The current Graph / Neighborhood freeze is accepted as 20 candidate rows. `SpaNiche` and `SpatialQuery` are official candidate rows in the current state, not registry-external exceptions. `Squidpy` is visible only as non-counted AnnData/scverse workflow context for spatial graph construction, neighborhood enrichment, and spatial graph utilities. It is not a method-table row, not ranked against the 20 dedicated candidates, and not runtime support, adapter availability, execution readiness, or default-method evidence.

Route to `Domain / Clustering` when the primary endpoint is hard tissue, domain, region, or spatial cluster labels. Route to `Cell-Cell Communication` when the primary endpoint is ligand-receptor edges, sender-receiver pairs, communication scores, pathway CCC, or causal communication interpretation. Colocalization, motifs, and neighborhood proximity should not be described as physical interaction or causal communication proof.

## Decision Tree

If the task asks for standard neighborhood graph construction, spatial graph utilities, neighbor enrichment, or an AnnData-based spatial workflow route:
- Use `Squidpy` as backbone/workflow context only.
- Do not count `Squidpy` as one of the 20 dedicated Graph / Neighborhood methods and do not rank it against them.

If the primary endpoint is hard tissue/domain/region labels or spatial clustering:
- Route to `Domain / Clustering`.
- Treat `SOTIP` as Graph / Neighborhood only when microenvironment modeling or differential microenvironment relationships are the endpoint.

If the primary endpoint is ligand-receptor edges, sender-receiver pairs, communication scores, neighbor-preference effects, pathway CCC, or causal communication interpretation:
- Route to `Cell-Cell Communication`.
- Keep `NicheCompass`, `CytoCommunity`, `SMORE`, `TrimNN`, `SpaNiche`, and `SpatialQuery` within Graph / Neighborhood only for niche, neighborhood, motif, topology, or colocalization-pattern outputs.

If the desired output is discrete niches or tissue cellular neighborhoods:
- Prefer / consider `CellCharter`, `CytoCommunity`, `scNiche`, or `SpaNiche` according to the available cell or spot features, annotation quality, and cohort comparison needs.

If the desired output is motifs or topology:
- Prefer / consider `SMORE`, `TrimNN`, or `SpatialQuery`.
- Carry graph construction, annotation, null-model, and preprint caveats for motif interpretation.

If the desired output is continuous factors, soft signatures, ecological metrics, or microenvironment relationships:
- Prefer / consider `NSF`, `mNSF`, `NNMF`, `MESA`, or `SOTIP` according to whether the branch is factorization, scalable overlapping signatures, ecological metrics, or microenvironment relationship modeling.

If the desired output is atlas-scale, foundation-model, disentangled, reference-assisted, reconstructed, or gene-level spatial context:
- Prefer / consider `CellNiche`, `Nicheformer`, `DECIPHER`, `ENVI`, `scHolography`, or `SIGEL` according to the reference, pretrained-model, reconstruction, and gene-representation requirements.

After choosing a branch:
- Keep niche, motif, factor, embedding, reconstruction, and gene-representation outputs distinct.
- Treat colocalization and motif findings as spatial association or organization signals, not as physical interaction, causal communication, or mechanism proof.
